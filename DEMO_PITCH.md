# Safety1271 Demo Pitch

**Target delivery:** 2 minutes 45 seconds  
**Presenter perspective:** Safety and security team  
**Demo order:** Recorded person-in-red-top scenario, then live drone view

## Spoken pitch

As part of our safety and security team, I’m always asking: while we’re monitoring one area, what might we be missing somewhere else?

A church campus can cover a lot of ground, but no one can watch every area at once. Fixed cameras are valuable, but each stays pointed at its installed view. Parked vehicles, building edges, and camera angles can leave areas out of view.

Safety1271 combines a drone camera with AI that looks for specific things and describes what is visible. It gives our team another perspective—not an automated decision-maker.

**[Play the pre-recorded “Person in a red top” scenario.]**

In this recorded example, we ask the system to find a person in a red top. It describes the person walking toward the red car and getting into it. The narration sticks to what is visible; it does not guess who the person is or why they got in.

**[Switch to “Live drone view” and show the live feed from the room.]**

Now I’m switching to the live drone view. This aerial perspective gives us a different view from a fixed camera. In a real patrol, an operator can check another part of the grounds or look from another angle, including areas outside a fixed camera’s view. It complements fixed cameras; it does not replace them. The drone still cannot see through roofs, walls, or dense obstructions, and it cannot remove every blind spot.

Other demo prompts include a car with its doors open, an unattended backpack, and free parking spaces. These are things to check—not proof of danger or wrongdoing. Staff review the information and decide what, if anything, to do.

Weather matters too. Drones have operating limits for temperature, wind, and precipitation. Our pre-flight workflow checks conditions such as wind, visibility, and precipitation to help the team decide whether to proceed or wait.

The prompt only changes what the AI looks for; it does not launch, move, or land the aircraft. Our team stays responsible for the aircraft and every response.

Safety1271 pairs a recorded example with a live aerial view to help our team notice what a fixed view may miss and make informed decisions—with people, not technology, at the center.

## Presenter cues

- Run the recorded person-in-red-top scenario first; let the narration finish before switching views.
- Then select **Live drone view** and explain the real-world value of a movable aerial perspective alongside fixed cameras.
- Keep the live feed genuinely live. If it is unavailable, say so and label any substitute footage as simulated.
- Do not describe an AI observation as a confirmed identity, threat, or motive.
- Weather checks inform the go/no-go decision; they do not make unsafe conditions safe or remove aircraft operating limits.
